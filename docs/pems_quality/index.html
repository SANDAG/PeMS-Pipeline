Raw Record Validity

2025 PeMS 5-Minute Interval Data

Before station-days are even assembled, individual five-minute records are dropped when they fail basic sanity checks (missing timestamp/station, non-positive sample counts, or missing traffic-flow values). This section measures that upstream loss directly by checking which filters result in records being dropped, out of 25,297,920 raw records ingested.

The two dominant conditions above, invalid sample counts and missing flow values, overlap: a record can fail both at once. The chart below shows how much each filter catches independently versus together.

508 of 1,440 stations (35.3%) have zero surviving records after filtering - they are entirely absent from every downstream table.

Beyond whether a record is valid, percent_observed measures how much of each five-minute reading is actual sensor data versus imputed/estimated. The chart below buckets every raw record (valid or not) by this value. No filter on percent_observed was adopted as previous filters already addressed most cases (see more analyses in the following section).

Completeness by Time Period

2025 PeMS

The chart below shows the number of station-days that do not contain the expected number of five-minute observations in each model time period. These are dropped in gold layer.

Incompleteness isn’t all-or-nothing: the chart below buckets every station-day by what share of its 288 expected five-minute intervals were actually present. Only 11.8% were dropped in this process.

Over that same population, the chart below buckets station-days by their average percent_observed - “100” means fully observed all day with no imputation; lower buckets rely on increasing amounts of estimated values. It is clear that most station-days have a high average percent_observed, indicating that the previous filters of (1) validity and (2) completeness of 5-min intervals were effective in ensuring high data quality.
